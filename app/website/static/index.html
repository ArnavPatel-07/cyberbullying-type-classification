Why cyberbullying matters

Cyberbullying is the use of digital platforms to harass, demean or threaten someone. Unlike a playground, it follows people home, spreads in seconds and can stay online for years.

24/7

It never switches off

Messages reach a person on their own phone, at any hour, in front of a large audience.

⚠

Real harm

Targets often report stress, anxiety, isolation and falling grades. Hate aimed at who someone is cuts deepest.

⚙

Too much to read

Platforms handle millions of posts a day. Automatic detection helps human moderators find the worst content first.

The six categories our model recognises

AgeAbuse targeting how young or old someone is.
EthnicityAbuse targeting race, colour or origin.
GenderSexist or gender-based harassment.
ReligionAbuse targeting someone's faith.
Other cyberbullyingInsults and harassment that fit none of the above.
Not cyberbullyingNormal, harmless messages.

If this is happening to you

  • Do not reply. Take screenshots with dates and usernames, then block the account.
  • Tell someone you trust: a friend, family member or teacher.
  • Report it in India: file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in, or call the cybercrime helpline 1930.
  • Children and teens: Childline 1098 is free and open 24/7. In an emergency call 112.

What we built

A full text-classification pipeline, from raw tweets to a deployed model.

1DatasetKaggle "Cyberbullying Classification": 47,692 tweets, 6 classes
→
2CleaningRemoved links, @mentions, duplicates and conflicting labels: 43,711 left
→
3SplitStratified 80/10/10 into 34,968 train, 4,371 validation, 4,372 test
→
4ModelsClassical ML, BiLSTM and four transformers
→
5EvaluateMacro-F1 on a test set used only once

Classical ML

TF-IDF (term frequency-inverse document frequency) features with Logistic Regression, Naive Bayes, SVM (support vector machine), Random Forest, XGBoost and a voting ensemble.

Deep learning

A BiLSTM (bidirectional long short-term memory) network with attention, averaged over 3 random seeds.

Transformers

DistilBERT, BERT, RoBERTa and BERTweet, each fine-tuned 3 times with different seeds.

Results

Test-set macro-F1: the average of the per-class F1 scores, so every category counts equally. Transformer scores are the mean of 3 runs.

Transformers tie

BERTweet, RoBERTa, DistilBERT and BERT land within about 0.5 points of each other, which is inside the test set's sampling error.

Small edge over classical

The best transformers beat the best classical model (LinearSVM with word and character features) by only about 1.2 points.

Why DistilBERT is deployed

It is about half the size and trains in about half the time, for roughly 0.2 points less than the top score.

Live demo

Educational demo only. The model makes mistakes, especially between "other cyberbullying" and "not cyberbullying". Never use it to make real moderation decisions about a person.

Limitations and ethics

Noisy labels

192 test tweets were misclassified by every model we trained. Many look mislabelled in the dataset itself, which caps the achievable accuracy.

English tweets only

The model has not seen Hinglish or other code-mixed text, which is common in India. This is our main future-work direction.

Humans stay in the loop

Sarcasm, reclaimed words and friendly banter confuse models. A human should review anything that affects a person.
